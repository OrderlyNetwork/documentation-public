# Overview

Trading fees are the costs you pay when you buy, sell, or trade on an exchange. On Orderly, fees are measured in **basis points (1 bps = 0.01%)** of your trade's total value.

Orderly Network uses a two-layer fee structure:

1.  **Base fees:** The cost the **Builder** (the DEX) pays to Orderly Network.
2.  **User fees:** The fee the **Trader** pays to the Builder.

### The dual-layer example

Here's how the two layers work together in practice:

- **Orderly base taker fee:** 3 bps (0.03%)
- **Builder custom fee:** 5 bps (0.05%)
- **Result:** The **Trader pays 5 bps**. Orderly retains **3 bps**, and the **Builder keeps 2 bps** as profit.

### Fee types

**Maker fees:** Charged when your order adds liquidity to the order book -- for example, a limit order that doesn't fill right away. Maker fees can be **negative** (rebates) for higher tiers, meaning you **receive** a rebate when your order adds liquidity.

**Taker fees:** Charged when your order removes liquidity by filling immediately -- for example, a market order.

**Hidden order fills:** When hidden quantity from a resting limit order is
matched by a new incoming order, the hidden side is marked as **Taker** and
charged the taker fee. If the incoming order is eligible to be maker, such as a
Post Only order that matches only hidden quantity, the incoming order is marked
as **Maker** and charged the maker fee.

**Liquidation fees:** If your position gets liquidated, a liquidation fee is applied. See the [liquidation fee](https://orderly.network) docs for details.

<Note>
  Perpetual futures trading fees are charged after every trade in USDC and are **factored into the
  position's average entry price**.
</Note>

# How fees work for Traders

As a Trader, you pay a single fee per trade that's set by the Builder (the DEX you're using).

- **Total fee paid:** You see one transparent fee per trade. This fee typically includes Orderly's base fee plus the Builder's margin.
- **How it's charged:** Fees are automatically deducted from your USDC balance when your trade executes.
- **Maker vs Taker:** Whether you pay the maker or taker rate depends on how your order is filled. If your order sits in the book and waits, you're a **Maker**. If it fills immediately, you're a **Taker**.

# How fees work for Builders

Orderly gives Builders full control over their pricing. You set your own trading fees while tapping into Orderly's shared liquidity.

- **Set your own fees:** You decide what to charge your users. The only rule: your fee must be at least as high as Orderly's **base fee**. The base fee is set per product type, so Orderly Predict has its own base fee; see [Orderly Predict fees](#orderly-predict-fees).
- **Keep your fee margin:** For taker fees, you retain **100% of the margin** between what you charge users and what Orderly charges you. For maker fees, positive fees collected from traders are Builder revenue. Protocol-funded maker rebates are passed through to traders and are not Builder revenue.
- **Base fee tiers:** The base fee Orderly charges you depends on your tier in the Builder Staking programme.

### Builder Staking programme

Builders can get lower base fees and better support by hitting monthly volume targets **or** staking \$ORDER tokens. **If you qualify for different tiers through both volume and staking, you automatically get the higher tier (lower fee).** Builders are entitled to the same Orderly base fees regardless of whether trades are on crypto or RWA markets. Builders can still configure different user-facing trading fees for crypto and RWA trades respectively. # Orderly Predict fees

For what Orderly Predict is and how a round works, see the [Orderly Predict Overview](/introduction/trade-on-orderly/orderly-predict/overview). Orderly Predict uses a different fee formula from perpetual futures and RWA markets, because the formula weights the traded price `p` (0.01 to 0.99) with a `p x (1 - p)` term rather than charging a flat rate. This section covers how the fee is calculated and how it splits between Orderly and the Builder.

### How the fee is calculated

Orderly Predict trades UP and DOWN shares. Every fill is priced and charged on its UP-book execution price, even for a DOWN trade:

```
fee per share = p x fee rate x (1 - p)
fee for the fill = shares filled x fee per share
```

- `p` is the UP-book execution price for that fill. A DOWN trade at price `d` is the same fill at UP price `p = 1 - d`; the formula always uses `p`, never a separate DOWN formula.
- `fee rate` is the account's Predict maker or taker rate, chosen by the role -- maker or taker -- of that fill. Which rate applies to you depends on the app you're trading on -- check your app's order screen to see your current rate. Unless the Builder you're trading through has customized it, the default is taker 300 bps, maker 0 bps.
- Fees are charged in USDC and calculated fill by fill. An order that fills across several prices is never charged on its average price.

<Note>
  Builders integrating via API can read an account's current rates from `binary_maker_fee_rate` and
  `binary_taker_fee_rate` on [`GET /v1/client/info`](/build-on-omnichain/restful-api/private/get-account-information)
  (bps, divide by 10000 to get the decimal rate used in the formula below).
</Note>

Because of the `p x (1 - p)` term, the fee is not a flat percentage of the fee rate:

- At `p = 0.50`, the fee per share is 25% of the fee rate -- the highest point on the curve.
- At `p = 0.10` or `p = 0.90`, the fee per share is 9% of the fee rate.
- Expressed against the traded notional (shares filled x `p`), the effective rate is `fee rate x (1 - p)`: it is highest when `p` is low and lowest when `p` is high, even though the fee amount itself is largest at `p = 0.50`.

#### Worked example (default taker rate, 300 bps)

Buying 100 shares at `p = 0.50`:

- Fee per share: `0.50 x 0.03 x 0.50 = 0.0075`
- Fee for the fill: `100 x 0.0075 = 0.75` USDC
- Trade notional: `100 x 0.50 = 50` USDC, so the fee is 1.5% of notional

Buying 100 shares at `p = 0.10`:

- Fee per share: `0.10 x 0.03 x 0.90 = 0.0027`
- Fee for the fill: `100 x 0.0027 = 0.27` USDC
- Trade notional: `100 x 0.10 = 10` USDC, so the fee is 2.7% of notional

The second fill has a smaller fee in USDC, even though it is a larger share of its (smaller) notional.

### Maker and taker rates

- Default user rates are **taker 300 bps, maker 0 bps**. Builders can adjust maker up from 0 bps, and can only adjust taker down, since the default taker rate already sits at the shared cap. Taker cannot go below the tier's Predict taker base rate; see [Custom Fee Structure](/build-on-omnichain/user-flows/custom-fees) for how.
- The Predict maker rate has a floor of 0 bps, and the Predict taker rate has a floor of the tier's Predict taker base rate. Unlike perpetual futures and RWA, Predict does not support negative rates or maker rebates.
- Predict maker and taker rates share the same upper cap of **300 bps (0.03), inclusive**. A Builder-set value above the cap is rejected when written.
- A maker rate of 0 bps is a valid configuration and means makers are charged exactly no fee.
- Settlement at expiry is performed by the system, not by matching, and is never charged a fee. Only trades -- opening a position or exiting it early with an opposite trade -- are charged.

### Predict base rate

Every Predict fill splits between Orderly's base fee and the Builder, using the same `p x (1 - p)` curve as the user-facing fee:

```
Orderly's share of the fill = shares filled x p x base rate x (1 - p)
```

- The base rate depends on the Builder's [Builder Staking programme](#builder-staking-programme) tier, using the same tier rules as other markets. See the "Predict taker base rate" row in each tier above.
- Predict maker base rate is 0 bps at every tier.
- Predict trading volume counts toward the Builder's 30-day aggregate volume tier threshold together with perpetual futures volume, counted as shares filled x the UP-book price `p` (the fill's notional) -- even for a DOWN fill -- not raw share count.
- Builders cannot set a Predict taker rate below the tier's Predict taker base rate, the same rule as other product types.
- If a Builder's tier drops and its Predict taker base rate rises, any existing Predict taker rate below the new base rate is raised to the new base rate automatically.
- Because the user-facing rate is always at least the base rate, the split between Orderly and the Builder is constant at every price `p`.
